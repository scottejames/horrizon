Plan and Do

Horizon separates planning from getting things done. You can only really work on things over a short window. Beyond that you can plan, but the plan should stay high level.

  • Do: Today and Tomorrow. The tasks you're actually working on. Only these can have progress notes. Keep this list short and clean. A task's project is shown, but faintly, because here the work matters more than where it fits.
  • Plan: Someday. Everything else. Capture as much as you like and organise it by project; it's fine for this list to be long. Plan tasks aren't scheduled and aren't worked on, but you can still rename them, change their priority or project, tick them off, or delete them.

To start working on something, move it to Today or Tomorrow. That's the moment you commit to it, and it's when it can start collecting progress notes.

The three horizons

Every task sits on exactly one horizon, shown as a tab across the top of the list:

  • Today: what you're committing to today.
  • Tomorrow: lined up for tomorrow.
  • Someday: your plan. No date, grouped by project.

The tabs are labelled Do (Today, Tomorrow) and Plan (Someday). There's no Next Week: anything that far out is planning, so it belongs in Someday. Tasks that were on Next Week have been moved to Someday.

Each horizon keeps its color everywhere in the app (tabs, buttons, dots), so you can tell where a task belongs at a glance. Color is used for nothing else.

Horizons don't roll over on their own yet. When tomorrow arrives, tasks on Tomorrow stay there until you move them to Today yourself.

Adding tasks

Type into Add to your list and press Enter. One line can carry everything about the task. Mix in any of the keywords below, in any order, and the preview under the box shows how they'll be read before you add it.

TypeSetsIf you leave it out
today tomorrow someday
(next week also means Someday)		 Horizon Today
!high !med !low
(or !h !m !l)		 Priority Medium
#CODE Project, by its short code No project
@personal @work Personal or Work The project's setting, or else whichever tab you're on

For example, Call tile supplier !high #KIT tomorrow @work adds “Call tile supplier” to Tomorrow, high priority, in the KIT project, under Work.

A #CODE you haven't used before is accepted rather than rejected: Horizon creates a new project with that code and puts it in Unassigned for you to organise later.

Working your list

  • Tick the checkbox to mark a task done. It stays on the list, struck through, and sinks below the tasks still to do. Tick again to undo.
  • Priority is the three-bar signal on each task: more filled bars means higher priority. Click it to change the priority.
  • Project chip: click a task's #CODE to open that project.
  • Progress notes (Today and Tomorrow only): click a task's 📝 button (it appears when you hover over the task) to open its notes. Type what's happened and press Enter. Each note is stamped with the date and time, and the newest shows first. Notes can't be edited or deleted once added. A task with notes always shows 📝 and how many it has. If you move it to Someday, its notes are kept and you can still read them; you can add more once it's back on Today or Tomorrow.
  • Search, filter and sort sit above the list. You can search descriptions, filter by priority or project (including “No project”), and sort by Priority, Alphabetical or Project. Whatever the sort, open tasks always come first, then deferred ones, then done ones. These settings apply across all three tabs and reset when you reload.

Rescheduling

Each task row shows two colored buttons, one for each horizon the task isn't on (Tdy, Tmrw, Someday). One click moves it there.

  • Moving later is a Defer. The task picks up a small “↩ deferred from …” tag and sorts below the open tasks on its new list. That's deliberate: if the same task keeps sliding, you'll see it.
  • Moving earlier is a Schedule. Pulling a task forward (for example, Tomorrow to Today, or anything out of Someday) clears any deferred tag. The task arrives as a normal open task.

Hover over a button to see whether it will defer or schedule.

Someday

Someday is your plan: things with no date. The list is grouped by project, with tasks that have no project at the bottom; click a project's name to open it. Nothing ever moves a Someday task onto Today or Tomorrow automatically. It leaves Someday only when you move it yourself.

While any Someday tasks exist, the sidebar shows an “N in Someday” box; click its → to jump to the Someday tab and review them. When Someday is empty, the box disappears.

Personal and Work

The Personal / Work switch in the header changes the whole app at once: the sidebar, the tabs, the lists and the counts. Every task, project and area belongs to one side or the other, independently. A task doesn't have to match its project.

You rarely need to set this by hand. New tasks follow the @work or @personal you typed, then their project's setting, then the tab you're on. New areas and projects follow the tab you're on.

Areas and projects

Areas of Responsibility

Areas are the standing parts of your life or work (Home, Health, Finances…), grouped by head space rather than any formal structure. They appear in the sidebar as a tree that you can expand and collapse. Horizon remembers which areas you left open.

Projects

  • Use the sidebar's + controls to add an area or a project. New projects land in Unassigned, and each one gets a short code (e.g. “Kitchen Remodel” becomes KR) for use with # in quick-add.
  • To file a project under an area, drag its row onto the area. Or, on a phone or with the keyboard, open the project and use Move to area. Only areas on the same Personal/Work side are offered.
  • Click a project (in the sidebar or on a task's chip) to open its panel. It slides in over your list without losing your place.

Inside a project

  • The panel lists every task linked to the project, across all horizons.
  • Its own add box is for brain-dumping: it stays open after each add. Tasks added there go to Someday unless you type a horizon. The main add box defaults to Today instead, because it's for things you mean to do now.
  • The Progress section is a running summary of what's been finished in the project (see below).

What happens to finished tasks

A done task stays visible for 24 hours, then it's deleted for good. If it belonged to a project, a line recording it is added to that project's Progress summary first, so the record isn't lost. A task's progress notes are deleted along with it and are not copied into the project's summary, so copy out anything you want to keep before then. Once a day the summary condenses older entries into a single running total, so it stays short.

This tidying only happens while Horizon is open. If you haven't opened it for a while, it catches up the next time you do.

Renaming and deleting

Hover over a task, project or area (or tab to it with the keyboard) to reveal its pencil and delete icons.

  • Rename: click the pencil, type, then press Enter or click away to save. Press Escape to cancel.
  • Delete always asks first and tells you exactly what will happen, such as “3 projects will move to Unassigned.” Yes is the default, so pressing Enter confirms.
  • Deleting never takes other things with it. Deleting an area moves its projects to Unassigned. Deleting a project unlinks its tasks, which stay on your lists.

Installing on your phone

Horizon can be installed like an app:

  • iPhone / iPad: in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen.
  • Android / Chrome: open the browser menu and choose Install app.

The installed app updates itself when a new version is released. It still needs a connection to load and save your tasks.

Dragging projects between areas doesn't work on touch screens. Use Move to area in the project's panel instead.
